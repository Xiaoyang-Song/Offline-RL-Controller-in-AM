Figure X. Effect of Jacobian-based uncertainty propagation on the surrogate's combined two-stage predictive uncertainty. The naive estimate adds the heating- and cooling-stage variances, $\Sigma_{\text{naive}} = \Sigma_{\text{heat}} + \Sigma_{\text{cool}}$. The propagated estimate first maps the heating-stage uncertainty through the Jacobian $J$ of the cooling-stage ensemble mean with respect to the end-of-heating field, $\Sigma_{\text{prop}} = (I+J)\,\Sigma_{\text{heat}}\,(I+J)^{\top} + \Sigma_{\text{cool}}$. All results use the surrogate trained on laser powers of 200–300 W and are evaluated on 500 test trajectories. (a–c) Per-node ratio $\sigma_{\text{prop}}/\sigma_{\text{naive}}$ of the total predictive standard deviation, with variances averaged node-wise over the test set, at layers 1, 6 and 12 (log-symmetric colour scale; dashed square: laser region of interest, ROI). Panel titles give the pooled ratio, $\sqrt{\sum \sigma^2_{\text{prop}} / \sum \sigma^2_{\text{naive}}}$, over nodes inside and outside the ROI. (d) Total $\sigma$ along the centreline ($y = 1.5$ mm) at layer 6. Shading marks the uncertainty removed by propagation, which is 23\% at the peak. (e) Pooled ratio inside and outside the ROI for every layer (solid: total; dashed: epistemic). Shaded bands are percentile-bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals over test trajectories (2000 resamples). (f) Inside-ROI ratio of the total $\sigma$ for each trajectory, grouped by whether the layer's laser power lies within the training range; lines show the median and bands the interquartile range. Propagation reduces the uncertainty where the laser acts and moves part of it into the surrounding material, consistent with conductive redistribution of heat during cooling. The reduction is largest for laser powers outside the training range, where ensemble disagreement about the overall heat input dominates the heating-stage uncertainty.
